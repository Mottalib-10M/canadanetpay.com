Nunavut Salary After Tax Calculator 2026

Calculate your take-home pay in Nunavut after federal tax, CPP, EI, and Nunavut provincial tax. Updated with 2026 NU tax brackets.

Nunavut has the lowest territorial tax rates in Canada across all four of its brackets, and a high basic personal amount, which together produce the lightest income tax burden of any jurisdiction in the country. That is a deliberate offset to a cost of living that runs far above the national average, where food and housing costs in many communities are several times those in southern Canada. The northern residents deduction applies at the highest prescribed-zone rate here, and for most residents it is the single largest item on the return. A comparison of take-home pay with a southern province therefore says very little without a cost-of-living adjustment. The prescribed-zone rate and the residency period conditions are set federally, not territorially, and they are claimed on the federal return.

What is specific to Nunavut

Tax here follows the standard structure: 4 provincial brackets applied on top of federal tax, with no surtax and no health premium. The whole difference with other provinces comes from where the thresholds sit and how steep the rates are.

Bear in mind that a difference in take-home pay is only one side of a relocation decision. The provinces that leave the most on a payslip are not always the cheapest to live in, and housing costs routinely swallow a gap of a few thousand dollars a year. The figures above answer the tax question precisely, and that question alone.

How the gap moves with salary

A province's ranking is not fixed across the income scale. Rates and thresholds interact, so a province that looks generous on a modest salary can look ordinary on a high one, and the reverse happens too. The table below shows Nunavut at four salary levels, with the difference against the average of all thirteen jurisdictions at each level.

Between the lowest and highest of those salaries, the average tax rate in Nunavut rises by 8.2 percentage points. That climb is the combined effect of the federal and provincial brackets, softened at the top by the pension plan and employment insurance ceilings, beyond which no further contribution is due. It is why the effective rate rises more slowly than the marginal rate at higher incomes.

Filing and residency in Nunavut

Provincial tax is determined by where you are resident on the thirty-first of December, not by where the income was earned or how the year was split. Someone moving to Nunavut in June is taxed by Nunavut on the whole year, with no apportionment. Payroll withholding follows the address your employer holds, so any difference between what was withheld and what is owed is settled in the annual return rather than at the moment of the move.

Residents of Nunavut file a single return covering both levels of tax, since the Canada Revenue Agency collects the provincial portion on the province's behalf. Only the schedule of rates and credits differs from one province to another; the filing process itself is identical.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Nunavut provincial tax brackets for 2026?
Nunavut has 4 tax brackets in 2026, ranging from 4.00% to 11.50%. The provincial basic personal amount is $18,767. These sit on top of the federal schedule, which is identical across the country, so the combined marginal rate is the sum of the two at each threshold. Each bracket applies only to the portion of income above its threshold, never to the whole salary.
What is the total tax rate in Nunavut?
Your total tax rate in Nunavut includes federal income tax (15%–33%), CPP (5.95%), EI (1.58%), and Nunavut provincial tax (4.00%–11.50%). Use the calculator above for your exact rate. None of these are a single flat percentage. Federal and provincial income tax rise in steps, while the contributions are proportional up to a ceiling and then stop entirely. The result is an effective rate well below the marginal one at every income level, and the gap between the two widens as salary rises.
How much tax do I pay on $75,000 in Nunavut?
On a $75,000 salary in Nunavut, your approximate take-home pay is $57,862/year ($4,822/month), with an effective tax rate of about 22.9%. That figure assumes no registered contribution and only the basic credits. An RRSP contribution reduces taxable income dollar for dollar, so its value equals the marginal rate at that point on the scale, which is the single largest lever available to a salaried employee here.
What is the Nunavut basic personal amount?
The Nunavut basic personal amount for 2026 is $18,767. This provides a non-refundable tax credit at the lowest provincial rate, effectively making this amount of income exempt from provincial tax. It works as a credit rather than a deduction, meaning it reduces tax already calculated rather than the income it is calculated on. The federal basic personal amount applies separately and at a different level, so the point at which any tax becomes payable depends on both figures together.
Does Nunavut have any additional taxes or premiums?
No. Nunavut charges provincial income tax and nothing else on employment income: no health premium, no surtax on the tax itself. The whole difference with other provinces therefore comes from where the bracket thresholds sit and how steep the rates are, which makes a comparison on published rates more reliable here than in provinces that layer extra charges on top.
