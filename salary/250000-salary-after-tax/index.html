Where the capital gains inclusion change matters most

At this income a realised capital gain is taxed at the top marginal rate on the included portion, and the inclusion rate itself has been the subject of recent change, with a higher rate proposed above an annual threshold for individuals. The distinction matters because it applies per year and per person rather than per disposition, which makes the timing and the splitting of a large realisation across years and across spouses the main available lever.

The second consideration is the interaction with the alternative minimum tax, which includes a larger share of capital gains in its base than the ordinary calculation. A single large disposition can therefore trigger the minimum tax even where the ordinary calculation would not, and the two effects compound rather than cancel.

A third element specific to this level is the treatment of investment losses. A capital loss can be applied against capital gains of the three preceding years or carried forward indefinitely, but never against employment income, which means a loss realised in a year without gains is a deferred asset rather than an immediate saving. The superficial loss rule denies the loss entirely where the same property is reacquired within thirty days by the taxpayer or an affiliated person, which includes a spouse and a registered account.
