The top federal bracket, and what stops working

The top federal bracket begins not far above this level, and from there the combined marginal rate exceeds fifty per cent in most provinces. Several planning routes that work lower down stop working here. The federal basic personal amount is reduced across the top bracket, so its value falls as income rises. And the alternative minimum tax, rewritten in recent years, now limits the combined use of the capital gains inclusion, donation credits and certain deductions.

What continues to work is registered contribution room, which is unaffected by income above the annual limit, and the timing of income recognition, since a deduction claimed in a year of higher income is worth more than the same deduction claimed in a lower one.

A third consideration is the interaction with childcare deductions, which are claimed by the lower-income spouse regardless of who paid them. In a household where one spouse earns at this level and the other substantially less, the deduction is therefore worth the lower marginal rate rather than the higher one, which is the opposite of the usual planning instinct and cannot be elected around.
