Minimum wage in Canada 2026: every province listed

Current minimum wage in every province and territory, the federal rate and who it covers, when each is reviewed, and what it works out to over a full year.

By Radif Partners, passionate about personal finance, Éditeur de calculateurs et de guides pratiques

Minimum wage in Canada is set provincially rather than federally, except for employees in federally regulated industries such as banking, telecommunications and interprovincial transport, who are covered by a federal minimum that applies wherever they work. The provincial rates differ by several dollars an hour and most are now indexed to the consumer price index, usually with an annual adjustment on a fixed date. Several provinces keep separate lower rates for specific categories, most commonly liquor servers, students under a given age and homeworkers, though a number have eliminated them. A rate quoted without a province and a date is therefore not usable for a payroll calculation. The guide lists the current rate for each province and territory with its indexation date, and notes which jurisdictions retain separate lower rates and for whom.

Current Minimum Wage Rates Across Canada

Minimum wage in Canada is set independently by each province and territory, with a separate federal rate that applies to federally regulated industries such as banking, telecommunications, interprovincial transportation, and the postal service. As of 2025, provincial minimum wages range from $15.00 per hour in Alberta to $19.00 per hour in Nunavut, reflecting significant regional differences in the cost of living and labour market conditions.

Most provinces adjust their minimum wage annually on either January 1 or October 1, often tying increases to the Consumer Price Index (CPI) to keep pace with inflation. Understanding these rates is essential for both workers and employers to ensure compliance with employment standards legislation.

Federal Minimum Wage

The federal minimum wage is $17.30 per hour as of April 1, 2025. It applies to employees in federally regulated sectors including banks, airlines, railways, telecommunications companies, Canada Post, and First Nations band councils. The federal rate is adjusted annually on April 1 based on the Consumer Price Index. If the provincial or territorial minimum wage is higher than the federal rate, the higher rate applies to federally regulated workers in that jurisdiction.

Provincial Minimum Wage Details

Ontario Minimum Wage

Ontario's general minimum wage is $17.20 per hour, effective October 1, 2024. The province reviews its minimum wage annually and adjusts it based on the Ontario CPI. Ontario eliminated the lower student minimum wage in 2022, so all workers regardless of age earn the same general rate. Liquor servers in Ontario now receive the full general minimum wage; the previous lower rate for liquor servers was eliminated on January 1, 2022.

British Columbia Minimum Wage

BC's minimum wage is $17.40 per hour as of June 1, 2024. BC adjusts its rate annually on June 1 based on the average percentage change in CPI. The province also eliminated the lower liquor server wage in 2021. BC has a separate rate for live-in camp leaders ($116.86 per day) and live-in home support workers (half the minimum wage rate for each hour of work or available time).

Alberta Minimum Wage

Alberta's minimum wage has remained at $15.00 per hour since October 1, 2018, making it the lowest general minimum wage in Canada alongside Saskatchewan. Alberta does not have a legislated schedule for future increases. Students under 18 who work 28 hours per week or less earn a youth minimum wage of $13.00 per hour. Alberta also eliminated the lower liquor server wage in 2016.

Quebec Minimum Wage

Quebec's general minimum wage is $15.75 per hour, effective May 1, 2024. The province adjusts its rate annually on May 1. Quebec maintains a separate lower rate for employees who receive tips (tipped workers), currently set at $12.60 per hour. This tipped wage is the lowest sub-minimum rate in Canada and applies primarily to restaurant servers, bartenders, and delivery workers.

Tipped and Liquor Server Wages

Most provinces have eliminated separate lower minimum wage rates for liquor servers and tipped employees. However, a few jurisdictions maintain distinct rates:

Ontario, BC, Alberta, Manitoba, and most other provinces have moved to a single minimum wage rate for all workers regardless of whether they receive tips. Quebec remains the notable exception with its lower tipped employee rate.

Youth and Student Minimum Wages

Only a few provinces have youth or student-specific minimum wage rates that differ from the general rate:

  • Alberta: $13.00 per hour for students under 18 working 28 hours per week or less.
  • Ontario: Students under 18 who work 28 hours per week or less during the school year previously earned a lower rate, but Ontario harmonized student wages to the general minimum in 2022.
  • Other provinces: Most provinces apply the general minimum wage to all workers regardless of age.

History of Minimum Wage Increases

Minimum wages across Canada have increased significantly over the past decade. In 2015, most provinces had minimum wages between $10.20 and $11.25 per hour. Alberta led one of the most dramatic increases, going from $10.20 in 2015 to $15.00 by 2018 under the NDP government's plan. Ontario saw major increases from $11.00 in 2015 to $14.00 in 2018, followed by a freeze, and then further increases to $17.20 by 2024. BC increased its minimum wage from $10.25 in 2015 to $17.40 by 2024 through annual CPI-linked adjustments.

The federal minimum wage was reintroduced in 2021 at $15.00 per hour after being dormant since 1996 (when it was set to match provincial rates). Since its reintroduction, the federal rate has been adjusted annually based on CPI, reaching $17.30 in 2025.

Minimum Wage and Take-Home Pay

Earning minimum wage does not mean you take home the full amount. Federal and provincial income taxes, CPP contributions, and EI premiums are deducted from every paycheque. For example, a full-time minimum wage worker in Ontario earning $17.20 per hour ($35,776 annually) would take home approximately $31,200 after deductions, depending on their personal tax credits and province of residence. Frequently asked questions

Which minimum wage applies to me?
The provincial or territorial one where the work is performed, in almost all cases. A small number of federally regulated sectors, including banking, interprovincial transport and telecommunications, follow the federal minimum instead, and where the provincial rate is higher the higher one applies. Students, liquor servers and home workers have separate rates in some provinces.
When do the rates change?
Most provinces adjust annually, commonly on the first of April or the first of October, and several index the change to the Consumer Price Index rather than legislating each rise. A few still set the rate by cabinet decision, which makes the timing less predictable. Any rate quoted without its effective date should be treated with caution.
What does the minimum wage work out to annually?
At a typical thirty-seven and a half hours a week it comes to somewhere between thirty and thirty-five thousand dollars a year before deductions, depending on the province. That sits below the first federal bracket threshold, so none of it is taxed at more than the lowest rate, and the basic personal amount shelters a meaningful share of it entirely.
Is the minimum wage the same as a living wage?
No. A living wage is a calculation of what covers actual costs in a specific community, produced by research groups rather than governments, and it typically exceeds the legal minimum by several dollars an hour. It carries no legal force. The gap between the two is largest in cities with high housing costs, which is where the calculation is most often published.
