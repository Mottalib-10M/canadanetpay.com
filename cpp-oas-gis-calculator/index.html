CPP, OAS and GIS Calculator 2026: your public pensions together

Your Canada Pension Plan, Old Age Security and Guaranteed Income Supplement added up for October to December 2026, with the way they affect each other.

Canada's public retirement income comes from three programs that do not follow the same logic. The Canada Pension Plan, or the Quebec Pension Plan, pays a pension earned through contributions on employment income. Old Age Security pays a flat amount based on years of residence. The Guaranteed Income Supplement tops up OAS for people with low income, and every dollar of other income, starting with CPP, reduces it. As a result, a CPP pension does not add its full value to a low-income retiree's budget: for a single person, each extra dollar of CPP can cost fifty to seventy-five cents of supplement. This calculator puts the three programs side by side for the current quarter, includes the recovery tax that applies to high incomes, handles couples and partial OAS pensions, and shows how much supplement is lost for every additional hundred dollars of monthly CPP, which is the number to know before choosing when to start CPP.

Three programs, three logics

Canada's public retirement income rests on programs that work in entirely different ways, and most surprises come from treating them as one.

  • CPP (or QPP in Quebec) is an earnings-related pension, financed by contributions on employment and self-employment income. Its amount depends on your contribution record and on the age you start, between 60 and 70. The contributions themselves are calculated on the CPP / QPP calculator, and the rules of the plan are explained in the CPP guide.
  • OAS is a flat pension financed from general revenue, set by years of residence in Canada after 18. It is not reduced by CPP or by any other income, except through the recovery tax at high incomes.
  • GIS is a supplement for OAS pensioners with low income. Every dollar of other income, CPP first among them, reduces it.

The interaction in numbers

Moving from $600.00 to $900.00 of CPP a month raises the total by $81.59, not $300.00: GIS falls from $730.90 to $512.49. Below about $875 of CPP a month, a single pensioner still receives part of the top-up and keeps only 25 cents of each additional dollar of CPP; above it, 50 cents. That is the reason a modest CPP pension can look almost invisible on the total, and the reason the start date of CPP deserves more thought for someone who expects GIS than for someone who does not.

The lag matters too. GIS is calculated on the previous year's income, so the first year after CPP starts, GIS is still computed on the lower income, and the reduction arrives in the following July. The calculator shows the steady state, once both years carry the same income.

Partial OAS and GIS

Newcomers who arrived in Canada as adults often receive a partial OAS pension. GIS compensates for the missing part of OAS, so for low incomes the two together reach the same total as for a person with a full pension. A single person with 20 years in Canada and $400.00 of CPP receives $381.25 of OAS and $1,262.15 of GIS. Once income passes the point where GIS stops, the partial pension is no longer made up.

What to enter

  • Your CPP or QPP retirement pension per month, as paid or as estimated in your My Service Canada Account. A CPP survivor pension or disability pension counts as well, since all of it is income for GIS.
  • Other taxable income per year: workplace pension, RRIF or annuity payments, interest, rent, foreign pensions.
  • Employment income separately, because the first $5,000 and half of the next $10,000 are exempt for GIS.
  • For a couple, your spouse's income without OAS, GIS or Allowance.

Each program has its own detailed calculator: OAS, GIS, OAS recovery tax and Allowance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can a single senior receive from CPP, OAS and GIS?
With no other income, OAS at $762.50 and the maximum GIS of $1,138.90 give about $1,901.40 a month in October to December 2026. Adding CPP raises the total, but by less than the CPP amount, because GIS falls as CPP rises. Even at the 2026 maximum CPP of $1,507.65 at 65, a single person with no other income keeps $209.49 of GIS.
Why does my GIS go down when my CPP goes up?
GIS is income-tested and CPP is income, while OAS is not counted. For a single pensioner, every extra $1 of CPP lowers the basic supplement by 50 cents, and the top-up by another 25 cents while it lasts. The calculator shows the GIS lost for each additional $100 of monthly CPP so that you can see the trade-off before choosing when to start CPP.
Should a low-income senior delay CPP to 70?
Often not. Delaying CPP raises it by 0.7% a month, but for someone who will receive GIS, a larger CPP later mostly replaces supplement that would have been paid anyway, and the CPP forgone in the meantime is lost. Starting CPP at 65, or even earlier, tends to suit people expecting substantial GIS. Check both cases in the calculator before deciding.
Is the total shown before or after tax?
Before income tax. CPP and OAS are taxable, while GIS and the Allowance are not. For most people receiving GIS, the basic personal amount, the age amount and the pension credit mean little or no federal tax is due, but provinces differ. The OAS recovery tax, a separate repayment for high incomes, is deducted when it applies.
Where do I find my CPP amount?
My Service Canada Account shows your contributions and an estimate of your CPP retirement pension at 60, 65 and 70. The maximum at 65 in 2026 is $1,507.65 a month, but most retirees receive much less because few people contribute at the ceiling for a full career. Quebec residents should enter their QPP pension, which Retraite Québec estimates in the same way.
Does this calculator work for couples?
Yes. Choose your marital situation and enter your spouse's income, CPP included. The calculator gives your own CPP, OAS and GIS, plus the Allowance paid to your spouse if they are 60 to 64. Each spouse's GIS is calculated on the couple's combined income, so run it a second time from your spouse's side to see the household total.
